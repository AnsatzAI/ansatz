\begin{abstract}
The inner loop of superconducting qubit design is electromagnetic simulation:
each geometry iterate requires a multi-conductor capacitance extraction whose
lumped-oscillator reduction yields the Hamiltonian parameters designers
actually target. We describe the v0 Ansatz engine, which accelerates this loop
with two coupled components. First, a forward surrogate trained on 1{,}934
experimentally validated Ansys Q3D capacitance matrices (the SQuADDS database)
predicts capacitance matrices at microsecond latency, roughly $5\times$ more
accurately than nearest-design lookup in distribution ($\capmapegbr$ vs.\
$\capmapenn$ mean absolute percentage error), with downstream coupling and
dispersive-shift errors of $\gerrgbr$ and $\chierrgbr$. Second, because every
data-driven predictor degrades outside its training hull, a \emph{routed,
verified} field-solver answers arbitrary designs: a cost-model router chooses
per instance among sparse-direct, AMG-preconditioned Krylov,
surrogate-initialized matrix-free Krylov, and hybrid pipelines, every pipeline
terminates in residual verification, and a runtime escalation monitor bounds
the cost of routing mistakes. On geometry distributions anchored to the same
validated design family, the routed solver runs at 1.0002$\times$ the
per-instance oracle with 100\% correct pipeline selection on held-out and
out-of-distribution designs, is never more than 0.4\% behind the best fixed
choice on any instance, and avoids the $2$--$30\times$ penalty of committing
to any learned pipeline and the $\sim$900$\times$ penalty of a fixed
HINTS schedule. Notably, the router's optimal policy in 2D is to decline the
neural pipelines entirely --- sparse direct factorization dominates this
regime, a negative result we report rather than obscure, and one the router
discovers automatically. We argue this instance-level, verification-first
reformulation of learned solver routing --- rather than per-iteration
classification or uncertainty-quantified surrogation alone --- is the form in
which neural acceleration becomes deployable in engineering design loops.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Superconducting quantum processors are designed in a loop whose inner step is
electromagnetic simulation. A designer adjusts planar geometry --- arm lengths
of a cross-shaped transmon island, the reach of a readout claw, etch gaps to
the ground plane --- and asks what Hamiltonian the physical chip will realize:
charging energy $E_C$, qubit frequency $f_q$, anharmonicity $\alpha$, readout
coupling $g$, dispersive shift $\chi$. The workhorse answer is a
multi-conductor capacitance extraction (Ansys Q3D in most labs) followed by a
lumped-oscillator-model (LOM) reduction~\cite{koch,minev}. Each extraction
costs minutes; design loops, frequency-allocation studies, and yield analyses
run hundreds to thousands of them. This loop, not the final sign-off
simulation, is where design time actually goes.

Neural surrogates promise millisecond predictions, and in distribution they
deliver (Section~\ref{sec:experiments}). The deployment problem is what
happens \emph{off} distribution: a surrogate extrapolating outside its
training hull fails silently, and in a domain where a misallocated qubit
frequency means a dead chip after a multi-week fabrication cycle, silent
failure is disqualifying. The established alternatives are unsatisfying in the
other direction: fixed hybrid schedules such as HINTS~\cite{hints} interleave
a neural corrector with classical relaxation on a rigid cadence, paying
surrogate costs whether or not they help; per-iteration learned routing over
solver ensembles~\cite{router} adapts more finely but optimizes iteration
progress rather than the quantity a practitioner buys hardware for:
wall-clock to a \emph{verified} answer.

This report describes the v0 Ansatz engine, built around two commitments.
First, \textbf{verification is unconditional}: every field the engine returns
satisfies a relative-residual tolerance on the target discretization, checked
directly, with a sparse-direct fallback on failure. Learned components can
only spend or save time; they cannot corrupt results. Second, \textbf{routing
is an economic decision made per instance}: a cost model predicts the
wall-clock of each available pipeline for the design, grid, and tolerance at
hand, the cheapest is chosen, and a runtime monitor escalates --- once,
warm-started --- if the observed convergence slope betrays the prediction.
The result is an engine whose worst case is the classical solver plus
milliseconds, and whose common case is substantially faster.

\section{Setting}
\label{sec:setting}

\paragraph{Device family.} We target the xmon-style transmon with readout
claw: a cross-shaped island in an etched pocket of a grounded plane, one arm
wrapped by a C-shaped coupler. This family is the backbone of the SQuADDS
validated design database~\cite{squadds}, which records, for 1{,}934 designs,
the geometry and the Ansys Q3D capacitance matrix (adaptively refined to
$0.1\%$). The database varies three parameters (cross length 90--420\,$\mu$m,
claw length 70--400\,$\mu$m, ground spacing 4.1--10\,$\mu$m); our generated
benchmark family varies all seven (widths, gaps included) around the same
anchors.

\paragraph{From fields to Hamiltonians.} With conductors
$\{1,\dots,K\}$ embedded as Dirichlet regions, excitation $k$ solves the
Laplace problem with conductor $k$ at unit potential; discrete Gauss-law
charges assemble the Maxwell capacitance matrix $C$. The LOM reduction maps
$C_q = |C_{\mathrm{cross,gnd}}| + |C_{\mathrm{cross,claw}}|$ and
$C_c = |C_{\mathrm{cross,claw}}|$ through exact Cooper-pair-box
diagonalization to $(f_q, \alpha, E_C)$ and through the exact lumped coupling
formula to $g$, with the beyond-RWA second-order dispersive shift
$\chi$~\cite{koch,squadds}. Designers target the Hamiltonian quantities; the
capacitance matrix is the interface our engine must produce quickly and
credibly.

\paragraph{Discretization.} v0 solves 2D plan-view electrostatics on uniform
grids ($n \in \{255, 511, 1023, 2047\}$, embedded Dirichlet masks,
matrix-free 5-point operators alongside assembled CSR forms). Verified
accuracy is defined against this discretization; the realism of the family
rests on the geometry anchoring above and on Tier~1 being trained on real Q3D
matrices. The 3D solver backend (AWS Palace) is roadmap, not v0.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Tier 1: forward surrogate on validated Q3D data}

Tier 1 is deliberately unglamorous: per-entry gradient-boosted regressors
mapping the varied geometry parameters to the five distinct capacitance-matrix
entries, trained on the 1{,}934 SQuADDS Q3D extractions. Inference is
microseconds on a CPU; retraining is seconds. We benchmark against the methods
a practitioner would otherwise use on the same database: nearest-design lookup
(the local behavior of database interpolation), distance-weighted $k$NN, a
global linear fit, and a random forest. SQuADDS's own published interpolation
is a physics-based scaling law around the nearest design~\cite{squadds}; no
forward ML surrogate ships with the database, which is precisely the gap
Tier~1 fills. Because designers consume Hamiltonian parameters rather than
capacitances, we report both the capacitance error and its LOM image
$(f_q,\alpha,g,\chi)$.

\subsection{Tier 2: routed, verified field solves}

Tier 2 answers the designs Tier 1 cannot be trusted on. The unit of work is a
\emph{design solve}: $K$ excitations sharing one geometry, hence one sparsity
structure --- so assembly, factorization, AMG hierarchy, and surrogate batch
inference are computed once per design and shared, exactly as a practitioner
would amortize them. The pipelines are:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{direct}: assemble once; sparse LU; $K$ triangular solves.
  \item \textbf{amg\_cg}: assemble once; smoothed-aggregation AMG setup
        (pyamg~\cite{pyamg}); CG per excitation, hierarchy reused.
  \item \textbf{surr\_cg}: U-Net field prediction per excitation (batched, one
        GPU pass); matrix-free CG from the predicted iterate; \emph{no
        assembly, no setup}.
  \item \textbf{surr\_amg}: surrogate init, then AMG-CG polish (buys AMG's
        convergence when the prediction is good but CG stalls).
  \item \textbf{hints}: the fixed 1-in-16 corrector schedule of~\cite{hints}
        with the same U-Net as corrector --- the fixed-schedule control.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Cost-model router.} Ten features describe an instance: the seven
geometry parameters, $\log_2 n$, free-node fraction, and $-\log_{10}\tau$.
Per-pipeline gradient-boosted regressors predict $\log$ wall-clock; the router
takes the argmin. Decisions cost microseconds, the model is a few hundred
kilobytes, and refitting on a customer's own instance mix is a
seconds-long job on measurements the engine logs anyway. We chose this over
per-iteration classification~\cite{router} deliberately: the object with
economic meaning is the total verified solve time, the per-design pipeline
space is small and structured, and instance-level regression makes the
router's errors interpretable (a mispredicted cost, not an opaque policy).

\paragraph{Escalation monitor.} Iterative pipelines log
$(t, \log_{10}\|r\|)$ per block; a linear projection of the recent slope
estimates the finish time. If the projection exceeds the predicted cost of the
best alternative (plus the elapsed segment), the solve escalates to that
alternative exactly once, warm-started from the current iterate. This converts
cost-model errors from unbounded stalls into a bounded, observable event.

\paragraph{Verification.} Every pipeline ends by evaluating the true relative
residual on the target grid. If it exceeds $\tau$ (never observed in our
benchmarks), the engine silently reruns the direct pipeline. The guarantee to
the user is therefore discretization-exact accuracy at $\tau$, independent of
every learned component.

\subsection{Guardrail properties}
\begin{proposition}[Verification safety]
Every field returned by any pipeline satisfies
$\|b - Au\|_2 / \|b\|_2 \le \tau$ on the target discretization; on
verification failure the engine falls back to the sparse direct solve, so the
routed result equals the classical result in the worst case.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[Bounded escalation regret]
Let $t_s$ be the time spent in a monitored pipeline before escalation fires,
and $T_{\mathrm{alt}}$ the (warm-started) time of the escalation target. The
routed cost is at most $t_s + T_{\mathrm{alt}}$, where $t_s$ is bounded by the
monitor's projection rule; in particular a stalled pipeline cannot consume
more than the patience window beyond its projected-competitive segment.
\end{proposition}

\paragraph{Headline numbers.} On 450 held-out test designs (plus 285 OOD
designs from ranges widened $1.3\times$), the router attains
\textbf{1.0002$\times$} (OOD: 1.0004$\times$) the per-instance oracle
wall-clock with \textbf{100\% oracle agreement}, at 11\,$\mu$s per decision in
sweep mode (0.62\,ms single-solve). Relative to fixed pipelines, routing is
never more than \textbf{0.4\%} (OOD: 0.6\%) slower than the best fixed choice
on \emph{any} instance, while being $2.1\times$ faster than AMG-CG
(geometric mean; $2.25\times$ OOD), $4.9$--$30\times$ faster than committing
to any surrogate pipeline, and $\sim$900$\times$ faster than the HINTS
schedule at the sizes where HINTS terminates within budget. Across all 5{,}930
measured solve cells there were \textbf{zero verification failures}; 758
budget-exhausted pipeline runs (mostly HINTS and unpreconditioned
surrogate+CG at large $n$) exercised the guaranteed direct fallback, whose
cost is included in their reported times.

\subsection{Discussion: the honest 2D verdict}
We expected a crossover --- direct at small $n$, surrogate-initialized
matrix-free iteration at large $n$. \textbf{It does not materialize in 2D},
and we report that deliberately. The etched-gap device family leaves only
$\sim$10\% of grid nodes unknown, so a $2047^2$ grid is a banded 2D system of
$\sim$400k unknowns that SuperLU factors in half a second; meanwhile every
matrix-free grid operation touches all $4.2$M nodes, a $10\times$ structural
handicap, and the surrogate's initialization advantage (1.6--1.9\% relative
error at native resolutions) buys too few iterations to overcome it. The
router's contribution on this workload is therefore \emph{refusal}: it learns
to decline the neural pipelines everywhere, ties the winner to within
microseconds, and converts the 2--900$\times$ penalty of a wrong fixed
commitment into a non-event. We regard ``the router correctly declines ML''
as a feature of verification-first design, not an embarrassment: the same
engine, contract, and cost model apply unchanged in 3D, where fill-in ends
sparse-direct dominance and memory-feasible solvers are exactly the
matrix-free, surrogate-initialized kind. HINTS' fixed cadence --- the same
corrector network, the same smoothers --- is dominated at every size
measured, confirming that \emph{when} to spend surrogate inference matters
more than \emph{whether}.

\section{Related work}

\textbf{Hybrid neural--classical solvers.} HINTS~\cite{hints} demonstrated
that a DeepONet corrector interleaved with relaxation damps complementary
spectral modes; its cadence, however, is a fixed hyperparameter, and our
measurements show the schedule is dominated by plain classical pipelines on
this workload at every resolution. The greedy PDE router~\cite{router} learns
\emph{which} operator to apply per iteration with approximation guarantees
under contractive error propagation; we inherit its central premise --- no
single method dominates --- but move the decision to the instance level,
target verified wall-clock, and add the escalation guardrail, trading
per-iteration optimality theory for deployability. \textbf{Design databases.}
SQuADDS~\cite{squadds} provides the validated data and a physics-based
scaling interpolation; its authors report percent-level accuracy in
distribution and degradation outside the pre-simulated hull, consistent with
what we measure for all data-driven predictors, including ours.
\textbf{Classical practice.} pyamg's smoothed-aggregation CG~\cite{pyamg} is
the strong open-source baseline for these SPD systems, and sparse direct
factorization is unbeatable at small scale --- both are therefore pipelines
inside the router rather than external competitors.

\section{Limitations and roadmap}

v0's field solver is 2D plan-view electrostatics: the right benchmark for
routing logic and for surrogate-initialized iteration, but not a replacement
for 3D extraction; Tier 1 carries the 3D realism today by training directly
on Q3D matrices. The near-term roadmap is (i) a 3D backend via AWS Palace
with the same router and verification contract, (ii) eigenmode analyses
(resonator frequency and $\kappa$) where the SQuADDS cavity-claw tables
provide validated anchors, (iii) cost-model transfer across hardware via
online refitting on engine logs, and (iv) uncertainty-aware Tier-1/Tier-2
handoff (hull-distance triggers) so the router also decides \emph{whether to
solve at all}. The engine logs every measurement needed to retrain both the
cost model and the surrogate, so each customer deployment improves its own
routing.
